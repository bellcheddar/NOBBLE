🧬 NOBBLE

Does a molecular glue actually change the interface it is supposed to create? NOBBLE measures it, against 285,996 real ones.

🌐 Websitemarcdeller.com ✉️ Contactmarc@marcdeller.com 🐙 GitHubbellcheddar/NOBBLE
🔬 Livenobble.mdeller.com

Neosubstrate and Oligopeptide Binder Benchmarking for Ligase Engagement. NOBBLE generates and scores peptide poses against curated targeted-protein-degradation (TPD) machinery using Boltz-2 ternary co-folding, and asks one question of every pair: does the presence of the glue or degrader change the predicted interface, and does that change separate experimentally degraded substrates from experimentally non-degraded ones assayed in the same screen?

Why it matters: a co-folding model will report high confidence in a ternary complex that does not exist, and it will do so in a way that looks exactly like success. NOBBLE's answer is not to trust the model's self-assessment but to compare every predicted interface against the distribution of 285,996 experimentally observed small-molecule bridges, to run a matched control for every positive, and to publish the pairs where the method fails as prominently as the ones where it works. It is useful for: triaging neosubstrate hypotheses before committing wet-lab effort, calibrating how far a co-folding model can be trusted on induced interfaces, and benchmarking glue-dependence against published degradation screens.

🎯 The headline metric

delta_iptm = iptm_min_ternary - iptm_min_binary

The difference in interface confidence between the ternary complex (E3 domain + peptide + small molecule) and the binary complex (E3 domain + peptide), taking the minimum across five seeds in each condition. Everything else in the pipeline exists to make that number trustworthy or to explain it.

Delta-ipTM is measured on the receptor-to-peptide chain pair, not the global ipTM: on a ternary complex the global figure averages in the receptor-to-ligand interface, which is the easy one, so a glue sitting perfectly in its pocket while the degron fails to dock would raise it.

🎯 Scoring

Seven signals, in descending order of trust. The composite is reported alongside its components, never instead of them, and its weights are fitted on the benchmark panel and the Sievers matched set rather than hand-set.

Signal What it is Status
Bridge percentile Buried surface, contact counts and bridging balance, as a percentile against 285,996 observed bridges primary
Delta-ipTM Glue dependence, receptor-to-peptide pair, minimum across seeds headline
Pose convergence Pairwise peptide backbone RMSD across seeds, superposed on the receptor working
ipTM-min Reported as enrichment, never as a verdict working
PLIP fingerprint Interaction pattern against the canonical degron contacts for that E3 class working
Lysine geometry Lysine exposure and distance to the glue reported as measurement, not validated
ProtCID packing veto Crystal-packing motifs flagged as furniture not computed, source unavailable

Two of those carry honest caveats rather than numbers, and both are documented in DECISIONS.md with the measurements behind them.

Two further things the table above would otherwise imply.

The status column is editorial and the measurements do not rank the signals that way. Every component's standalone AUC over the 158 labelled pairs sits between 0.52 and 0.59, and the one labelled headline is the lowest of the five. The app shows each component's measured AUC beside its fitted weight rather than the word.

Fitting the weights buys nothing measurable. The composite reaches AUC 0.6988 on the pairs the grid searched and 0.58 to 0.61 across held-out folds, against 0.5889 for the best single component taken alone. A single component carries no optimism, because nothing was fitted to produce it, so that is a fair comparison and the in-sample figure is the whole of the apparent gain. Thirty-one positives, with per-fold values from 0.0 to 0.84, so no interval is quoted. tools/composite_crossval.py.

📊 Early results

From the benchmark panel, at tier-1 triage settings, written up in FINDINGS.md:

  • Geometry separates poses that ipTM cannot. For GSPT1 / CRBN / CC-885, a pose that bridges scores ipTM 0.885 and one that does not scores 0.877. The highest ipTM in the ternary set, 0.916, does not bridge at all.
  • The glue docks reliably; the degron does not follow. All five canonical CRBN contacts (W380, W386, W400, H378, F402) matched in every seed, so CC-885 is placed correctly and reproducibly, while only 3 of 10 ternary trajectories bring the degron into contact with it. The PLIP fingerprint and the buried surface filter reach that verdict independently.

  • One trajectory in five failed to dock the peptide at all, leaving it 81 A from the receptor in free solvent. A confidence aggregate cannot tell that apart from a weak interface, so NOBBLE checks docking explicitly.

  • The 15-mers failed because a fragment cannot fold, and the fix is measurable twice. Submitting each substrate as its whole folded domain instead of a 15-residue window moves GSPT1 / CRBN from 29.22 A median pose RMSD to 1.17 A and BRD4 / VHL from 19.23 A to 1.71 A: correct poses, not improved ones. The IKZF1 control keeps its 15-mer in both runs and moves by exactly 0.00 A, so the change tracks the construct and nothing else.

  • A second, independent measurement agrees. Bridge formation asks only whether the glue touches both chains inside the prediction, with no crystal involved. GSPT1 goes from 3 of 10 seeds bridging to 10 of 10, BRD4 / VHL from 4 of 10 to 10 of 10, and not one seed in the corrected campaign fails to bridge. Every failed bridge in the original panel failed the same way: the glue reaching one chain rather than two, which is a placement failure rather than a weak interface.

The corrected-construct comparison is now complete: 7 pairs compared, none incomplete, 6 improved by more than 2 A, the control held and none got worse. Bridge formation agrees and needs no crystal, with four pairs bridging in more seeds and none in fewer. CDK12 was the last to land, moving from 44.92 A to 5.28 A and from 0 of 10 seeds bridging to 6 of 10.

These remain triage-tier numbers from one arm of the campaign. They are published here because a pipeline that only reports its successes is not a benchmark, and because of the two predictions recorded before those pairs ran, one is supported and one turned out wrong (FINDINGS.md, F18; tools/check_prediction.py scores them mechanically rather than by a reading after the fact).

📉 The main campaign, and why it is not published

The full campaign has since finished: 158 pairs, 2,360 jobs, scored, with both computable validation layers computed. By the same principle as above, here is what it says.

  • The matched-control ROC is a null. AUC 0.4583 over 24 degraded and 96 matched non-degraded zinc fingers from the same screen, which the code reads as "not distinguishable from chance at 95 per cent confidence". It holds at 0.4147 on the companion statistic a collapsed seed cannot inflate, so it is not an artefact of one bad draw.
  • Pose recovery fails on this campaign, which submits each substrate as a 15-mer: 0 of 65 poses within 3 A, median 17.54 A, and tier-2 refinement sharpens the poses without recovering them. A Delta-ipTM that cannot separate glue from matched control is indistinguishable from a Delta-ipTM computed over poses in the wrong place, so the null above cannot be read on its own.
  • The same measurement passes on the whole-domain campaign: 35 of 65 poses within 3 A at a median of 2.494 A, against none and 17.54 A for the 15-mers. So the recovery failure belongs to an input representation rather than to the method, and the atlas is built from the campaign where it fails.
  • The primary signal is right against crystals, on the same arm. Spec 4.1's score is a bridge geometry, and it had only ever been checked against its own output. Run on the seven deposited ternaries instead, it finds the bridge in six of six that model their glue; the seventh, 6BNB, is titled as a PROTAC complex and contains no PROTAC, so it cannot be asked. With the substrate supplied as its folded domain the measured buried area lands within 5 per cent of the deposited area on all six. With a 15-mer the glue still sits correctly in the E3 pocket and the substrate side collapses, so CDK12's bridge rate of zero is the representation rather than a blind filter. tools/bridge_on_crystals.py --against-predictions.
  • The collapse is not blanket, and that cuts against the null rather than for it. Sorted by how much the substrate buries in the crystal, a 15-mer reproduces 0.87 to 0.93 of it where the deposited interface is 215 to 330 square angstroms, and 0.26 to 0.29 where it is 528 or more: fifteen residues cap the substrate side at about 300 whatever the real interface is. The matched-control arm is a zinc-finger screen, and the one benchmark pair of that class, IKZF1's ZF2 degron, buries 228 and is reproduced at 0.928. So for the substrates the control arm is made of, the representation reproduces the deposited interface and under-burial is not available as an excuse for the null. Six pairs, one of the relevant class, and no Sievers substrate has a crystal to check against.
  • Neither result is prospective. All seven benchmark depositions predate the model's 2021-09-30 training cutoff, so the time-split layer reports no blind set at all. The failure is therefore not explained by novelty, which strengthens the representation reading; the success is on structures the model may have memorised, so it is consistent with recall as much as with prediction. Settling that needs a post-cutoff deposition, not more compute.

Publication is therefore held (DECISIONS.md, D-NOB-076), and held by the scripts rather than by a note: config/publication_hold.toml is read by tools/publication_hold.py as the first pre-flight step of deploy/deploy.sh. A missing hold file reads as held, and releasing it has to name the authorising decision. The site serves the earlier benchmark bundle until that is decided. No success radius was invented to make the control pass, and a test asserts none appears in config/thresholds.toml.

🧪 The design arm

A seventh tab, scoped in DESIGN_ARM.md and deliberately not part of the build specification. Everything else in NOBBLE measures whether a predicted interface tells you something true; this is the part that proposes something new.

It ships before the score that would rank it is validated, and that shaped it. When this was built the matched-control ROC reported not_computed; the Sievers arm has since run and the answer is a null, which makes the same decision firmer rather than softer. A tab that ranked invented peptides by a composite built on a statistic that cannot separate glue from matched control would be the failure the rest of the project exists to avoid: a ranked list reads as a recommendation whatever the caption says.

Property How it works
Anchoring Every variant descends from a parent carrying an experimental label. Nothing is scored in the abstract
Headline number Design lift, delta_iptm(variant) - delta_iptm(parent): a difference of differences, cancelling the receptor, the glue and the parent degron
What it claims The ordering of positions, which survives a monotone miscalibration. "Position 7 matters more than position 3" is durable in a way "this peptide will be degraded" is not
When it refuses Different construct, E3, ligand or modality, or fewer than three seeds on either side, and no lift is returned at all
Cost An alanine scan of a 15-mer is 15 variants and 225 jobs, about 4.9 hours. Saturating every position is 285 variants and 4,275 jobs, so saturation requires explicit positions

Two details that exist because the obvious implementation is wrong:

  • A variant inherits its parent's cofactor rather than re-deriving it. An alanine scan of a zinc finger mutates the coordinating cysteines, so inferring per variant drops the zinc at exactly the positions the scan exists to interrogate. The lift there would be large, would read as "this position is critical", and would be reporting the missing ion.
  • A position already alanine is scanned to glycine, not emitted unchanged. An unchanged variant costs 15 jobs to learn nothing and would read on the chart as "does not matter" when it was never tested.

🚀 Usage

# Phase 0: probe the machine and derive the tuning
python3 tools/hwprobe.py

# Phase 1: check the gates (panel resolution, degron recovery, token budget)
python -m pytest tests/test_phase1.py -q

# Phase 1: precompute one MSA per receptor construct, once, offline
bash tools/run_msa.sh

# Phase 2: assemble the benchmark campaign and run its queue
python -c "from nobble.execute.campaign import assemble_benchmark; print(assemble_benchmark())"
bash tools/run_queue.sh

# Phase 3: score whatever has landed, re-runnable against a live queue
python -m nobble.score.run

Long runs belong in tmux: tools/run_queue.sh wraps the queue in caffeinate -dimsu and the queue itself is resumable, because multi-day MPS runs hit OOM and NaN diffusion steps and a queue that cannot resume mid-run is unusable.

🌐 Deployment

The droplet serves a precomputed, read-only slice: the scored records and the predicted structures, with no folding and no scoring at request time. That is a hard constraint rather than an optimisation, and the deployment proved why: two routes imported the curation stack to render, passed every local check, and returned 500 in production because the droplet has no gemmi. The slice is built by deploy/build_atlas.py, which also withholds the PROTAC-DB chemistry, because publishing to a public site is redistribution exactly as a public repository is.

Script Does
deploy/survey.sh Read-only survey of the host, so conventions are read rather than assumed
deploy/provision.sh One-time, idempotent: service user, systemd unit, nginx vhost
deploy/certbot.sh TLS certificate, kept separate because it is rate-limited
deploy/build_atlas.py Builds and redacts the published slice
deploy/deploy.sh Syncs, fixes ownership before pip, restarts, verifies every page
deploy/repair_perms.sh The one to reach for when every page 500s but /healthz is fine

📄 Licence and reuse

The code in this repository is MIT licensed. The data is not ours to give. NOBBLE reads curated third-party databases through BINMAN, several of which restrict redistribution: PROTAC-DB is internal-use only, UbiBrowser ships as derived counts, and BioLiP2 annotations are not redistributed. No third-party dataset is bundled here, and the generated campaign artefacts are deliberately excluded from version control because some of them embed those records. If you reuse this, acquire the sources yourself under their own terms and cite them.
